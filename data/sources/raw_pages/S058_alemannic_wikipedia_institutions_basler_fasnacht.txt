=== SWISS FACT CONTENT ===
F1. The Basel Fasnacht is the largest carnival in Switzerland. — cross-ref: none
F2. The Basel Fasnacht begins on the Monday after Ash Wednesday at exactly 4 a.m. with the Morgestraich. — cross-ref: none
F3. The Basel Fasnacht lasts exactly 72 hours and ends on Thursday morning at 4 a.m. with the Ändstraich. — cross-ref: none
F4. Basel residents call these the "three most beautiful days" ("die drey scheenschte Dääg") and regard the Fasnacht as the most important event of the year. — cross-ref: none
F5. There are about 18,000 active Fasnacht participants (Fasnächtler), each wearing a costume (Goschdyym) and a mask (Larve). — cross-ref: none
F6. The Basel Fasnacht has been organised by the Fasnachts-Comité, its official negotiating partner, since 1911. — cross-ref: none
F7. In 2004 over 480 units were registered with the Fasnachts-Comité: 141 cliques and groupings, 141 floats (Wägen) and chaises, 61 Guggen (brass bands), 86 fife-and-drum groups and 55 single masks and small groups. — cross-ref: none
F8. That gives over 12,000 participants in organised groups, plus about 6,000 "wild" unregistered Fasnächtler not affiliated to any clique. — cross-ref: none
F9. The earliest mention of the Fasnacht is from 1376, the "Böse Fasnacht" (Bad Carnival), which brought great misfortune on the city. — cross-ref: none
F10. In 1529 the carnival period was fixed to the three days from Monday to Wednesday after Ash Wednesday — the reason Basel Carnival falls a week later than elsewhere. — cross-ref: none
F11. At the 1540 muster, 600 young men in armour with sword and halberd marched through the city, after which the city-quartier processions were established. — cross-ref: none
F12. The Basel Fasnacht is today regarded as the only large Protestant carnival in the world. — cross-ref: none
F13. The Fasnachts-Comité issues a badge (Plaggedde) in four metal grades: copper (9), silver (18), gold (45) and the "Bijou" (about 100). — cross-ref: none
F14. The largest Fasnacht in the canton Basel-Landschaft is that of Liestal, drawing tens of thousands of spectators. — cross-ref: none
F15. The Basel Fasnacht is not only a city affair — the Baselbiet (Basel-Landschaft) Fasnacht takes place in parallel, and in the Schnitzelbängg the country-dwellers outnumber the city people. — cross-ref: none
