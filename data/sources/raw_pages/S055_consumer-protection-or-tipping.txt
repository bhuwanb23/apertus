=== FACT CANDIDATES (for MCQ generation) ===
F1. TIPPING IS NOT OBLIGATORY in Switzerland. This is stated outright by Switzerland Tourism (the national tourism organisation): the guest is not obliged to tip, although paying one is customary in many restaurants, at roughly 10 percent or by rounding up. — evidence quote: "In Switzerland, the guest is not obliged to tip. In many restaurants, however, it is customary to pay a tip. This amounts to approximately 10 percent or it is rounded up to a round amount." [myswitzerland.com]

F2. The customary amount, where a tip is given, is approximately 10 percent or a round-up to a round figure. — evidence quote: "This amounts to approximately 10 percent or it is rounded up to a round amount." [myswitzerland.com]

F3. The reason a tip is optional is that service is already included in the price in the hospitality sector. — evidence quote: "The catering industry is one of those sectors in which service is actually included in the price. Nevertheless, some customers add a tip when paying for good service." [swissinfo.ch] / "Im Gastgewerbe ist das Trinkgeld seit 1974 im Preis inbegriffen." [gastrosuisse.ch, DE — translation: in the hospitality industry the tip has been included in the price since 1974]

F4. Under the current rules, VOLUNTARY TIPS ARE GENERALLY EXEMPT FROM TAX AND SOCIAL SECURITY CONTRIBUTIONS. But if a tip forms an integral/substantial part of an employee's salary it is treated as income and is subject to tax and social security deductions. — evidence quote: "Under current rules, voluntary tips are generally exempt from tax and contributions. However, if a tip forms an integral part of an employee's salary, it is treated as income and is therefore subject to tax and social security deductions." [swissinfo.ch]

F5. The law does NOT define what counts as a "substantial part" of salary — the Swissinfo report explicitly characterises this as a legal grey area / legal uncertainty. (Note: this is a news characterisation; the underlying statute was not retrievable.) — evidence quote: "The law does not clearly define what constitutes a "substantial part" of salary." / "This lack of clarity creates legal uncertainty, says Beat Imhof, president of GastroSuisse." [swissinfo.ch]

F6. There is NO generally applicable rule on how tips are distributed among staff; operational practice varies from establishment to establishment. Source is the trade association (GastroSuisse), German. — evidence quote: "In manchen Restaurants werden die Trinkgelder unter den Mitarbeitenden aufgeteilt. Allgemein gültige Regelungen gibt es nicht, die betriebliche Praxis ist unterschiedlich." [gastrosuisse.ch, DE — translation: in many restaurants tips are shared among the staff. There are no generally applicable rules; operational practice differs.]

F7. The hospitality trade association's stated legal position is that tips are neither wages nor part of wages. This is the association's own view and is contested by labour-law academics — it must NOT be presented as settled Swiss law. — evidence quote: "Grundsätzlich sind Trinkgelder kein Lohn und auch kein Bestandteil davon." [gastrosuisse.ch, DE — translation: as a rule, tips are neither wages nor part of wages.]

F8. Government position as of 6 March 2026: the Federal Council has described the situation as unsatisfactory and intends to clarify the definition of a "substantial part of salary"; the Interior Minister wants to examine whether electronically paid tips should in principle be subject to tax and contributions; the decision now rests with PARLIAMENT (Senate debate scheduled for early March 2026). The stated objective is social security coverage, especially in low-wage sectors. — evidence quote: "In a statement, the government described the current situation as unsatisfactory and said it intends to clarify the definition of a "substantial part of salary"." / "Interior Minister Elisabeth Baume-Schneider wants to examine whether electronically paid tips should in principle be subject to tax and contributions." / "Whether tips should remain tax-free or become fully subject to contributions will now be decided by parliament." / ""The main objective is to ensure employees' social security coverage and to make sure that every franc earned is included in pension calculations, particularly in low-wage sectors," the government wrote." [swissinfo.ch]

F9. The Federal government's own estimate of tipping volume is up to CHF 1 billion per year, while acknowledging that nobody knows the exact figure. — evidence quote: "There is plenty of tipping in Switzerland. The federal government estimates an immense amount: up to CHF1 billion ($1.29 billion) per year. But nobody knows exactly how much." [swissinfo.ch]
