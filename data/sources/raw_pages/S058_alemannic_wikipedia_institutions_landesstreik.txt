=== SWISS FACT CONTENT ===
F1. The Swiss general strike of 1918 (the Landesstreik) took place from 11 to 14 November 1918. — cross-ref: S037
F2. Over a quarter of a million workers and union members struck in various regions of the country. — cross-ref: none
F3. The Landesstreik is regarded as the most important socio-political confrontation in Swiss early-20th-century history. — cross-ref: none
F4. The Oltener Aktionskomitee action committee was formed in February 1918 and was led by National Council member Robert Grimm. — cross-ref: none
F5. On 6 November 1918, out of fear of revolution, the Federal Council called up a massive military contingent. — cross-ref: none
F6. Ultimately some 400,000 workers struck their workplaces. — cross-ref: none
F7. The strike demands included: election of the National Council by proportional representation, women's suffrage, introduction of a duty to work, the 48-hour week, reorganisation of the army into a people's army, expansion of food supply, old-age and disability insurance, nationalisation of import and export, cancellation of state debts through taxation, and a state monopoly on import and export. — cross-ref: S040
F8. Factories, the public administration, the railways and the newspaper printing works stood still in most Swiss localities. — cross-ref: none
F9. The Federal Council summoned the Federal Assembly to an extraordinary session and demanded an immediate end to the strike. — cross-ref: S002
F10. General Ulrich Wille put Zurich, Bern and other centres under military control; three workers were shot dead in Grenchen on 14 November. — cross-ref: none
F11. The first memorial to the Landesstreik was unveiled on 11 November 2008, its 90th anniversary. — cross-ref: none
F12. The strike's political effect was the switch from majority to proportional representation in the National Council elections of October 1919, in which the Social Democrats more than doubled their mandate from 20 to 41 seats. — cross-ref: S003
F13. The new factory act adopted the 48-hour week, and most of the other demands were subsequently implemented democratically. — cross-ref: S032
