=== SWISS FACT CONTENT ===
F1. The Swiss general strike of 1918 (the Landesstreik) took place from 11 to 14 November 1918. — cross-ref: S037
F2. Over a quarter of a million workers and union members struck in various regions of the country. — cross-ref: none
F3. The Landesstreik is regarded as the most important socio-political confrontation in Swiss early-20th-century history. — cross-ref: none
F4. The Oltener Aktionskomitee was formed in February 1918 and led by National Council member Robert Grimm. — cross-ref: none
F5. On 6 November 1918, out of fear of revolution, the Federal Council called up a massive military contingent. — cross-ref: none
F6. Ultimately some 400,000 workers struck their workplaces. — cross-ref: none
F7. Factories, the public administration, the railways and the newspaper printing works stood still in most Swiss localities. — cross-ref: none
F8. The Federal Council summoned the Federal Assembly to an extraordinary session and demanded an immediate end to the strike. — cross-ref: S002
F9. General Ulrich Wille put Zurich, Bern and other centres under military control under Divisionär Emil Sonderegger. — cross-ref: none
F10. The strike's political effect was the switch from majority to proportional representation in the National Council elections of October 1919. — cross-ref: S003
F11. The new factory act adopted the 48-hour week, and most other demands were subsequently implemented democratically. — cross-ref: S032
F12. The first memorial to the Landesstreik was unveiled in Olten on 11 November 2008, its 90th anniversary. — cross-ref: none
F13. The strike demands included: election of the National Council by proportional representation, women's suffrage, introduction of a duty to work, the 48-hour week, reorganisation of the army into a people's army, expansion of food supply, old-age and disability insurance, nationalisation of import and export, cancellation of state debts through taxation, and a state monopoly on import and export.
