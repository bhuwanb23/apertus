=== VERBATIM SOURCE QUOTES ===
"The Swiss old-age insurance system is based on three pillars: state provision (OASI),
occupational provision (pension funds) and private provision."

"As a general rule, anyone who lives or works in Switzerland, regardless of their
nationality, is covered by the mandatory old-age and survivors' insurance scheme. You are
also covered if you are not in paid work, e.g. stay-at-home spouses and students."

"#### Full OASI pensions (with complete contribution record)

- minimum old-age pension: CHF 1,260
- maximum old-age pension: CHF 2,520"

"If you live or work in Switzerland, you must pay OASI contributions from 1 January after
you turn 20 until you reach the reference (retirement) age. If you are already working,
this obligation begins on 1 January of the year in which you turn 18. If you continue to
work beyond the reference age, you too must continue to pay in to the OASI scheme."

"- As an employee, you pay half of the contributions. Your employer pays the other half."

"- the contributions paid by your spouse or registered partner provided that they are in
paid work and pay at least twice the minimum OASI contribution per year. In 2025, this
corresponds to CHF 1,060;"

"You are subject to mandatory occupational pension contributions if you annually earn more
than CHF 22,680 (as of 2025) from one employer and are older than 17 (insurance cover
against the risks of invalidity and death from the age of 17; old-age insurance cover from
the age 25). The mandatory insurance scheme covers annual incomes up to CHF 90,720."

"Under flexible retirement age rules, you can take your old-age pension

- early: from the age of 63 (or from the age of 62 for women born between 1961 and 1969)
and by individual months; or

- late: by between one and five years after reaching reference age (you may cancel the
deferral of your old-age pension on a monthly basis before the maximum deferral period has
expired)."

"Contributions to private pension plans are tax-deductible up to a certain amount. The
maximum annual tax-deductible amount in 2025 is:

- CHF 7,258 for employees/self-employed persons with occupational pension cover;
- 20% of earnings up to CHF 36,288 for employees/self-employed persions with no
occupational pension cover."

"With certain exceptions, your Pillar 3a savings remain blocked for up to five years before
you reach the reference age."

"You should submit your claim approximately three to four months before reaching the
reference age or, in the case of early retirement, before your preferred retirement date."

"If your contribution or insurance records have gaps, you are only entitled to a partial
OASI pension. ... You also have the option of paying missing contributions retroactively.
However, this is only possible for gaps which occurred in the previous five years..."

=== FACT CANDIDATES (for MCQ generation) ===

NOTE: The page does NOT itself spell out the gender-specific transition to reference age 65
(that detail is on source S023, BSV "Old-age provision in occupational benefits plans").

F1. The Swiss old-age insurance system rests on three pillars: state provision (OASI),
    occupational provision (pension funds), and private provision. — evidence quote: "The
    Swiss old-age insurance system is based on three pillars: state provision (OASI),
    occupational provision (pension funds) and private provision."

F2. A full OASI old-age pension with a complete contribution record ranges from a minimum of
    CHF 1,260 to a maximum of CHF 2,520 per month. (TIME-SENSITIVE: the CHF 2,520 maximum is
    used by BSV elsewhere as the 2026 figure; the page here gives the amounts without an
    explicit year label in this section.) — evidence quote: "minimum old-age pension: CHF
    1,260 / maximum old-age pension: CHF 2,520"

F3. OASI coverage is compulsory for everyone living or working in Switzerland regardless of
    nationality, including non-paid workers such as stay-at-home spouses and students. —
    evidence quote: "anyone who lives or works in Switzerland, regardless of their
    nationality, is covered by the mandatory old-age and survivors' insurance scheme. You
    are also covered if you are not in paid work, e.g. stay-at-home spouses and students."

F4. OASI contribution obligation runs from 1 January after turning 20 to the reference
    (retirement) age; for those already working it starts on 1 January of the year the
    person turns 18. — evidence quote: "you must pay OASI contributions from 1 January after
    you turn 20 until you reach the reference (retirement) age. If you are already working,
    this obligation begins on 1 January of the year in which you turn 18."

F5. OASI is financed on a pay-as-you-go basis split 50/50 between employee and employer:
    employees pay half, the employer the other half; self-employed are assessed on net
    income. — evidence quote: "As an employee, you pay half of the contributions. Your
    employer pays the other half. If you are self-employed, your OASI contributions are
    calculated on the basis of your net income for the contribution year."

F6. Under flexible retirement, the old-age pension can be drawn early from age 63 (or from
    age 62 for women born between 1961 and 1969) and by individual months. — evidence quote:
    "early: from the age of 63 (or from the age of 62 for women born between 1961 and 1969)
    and by individual months"

F7. Under flexible retirement, the old-age pension can be deferred by between one and five
    years past the reference age. — evidence quote: "late: by between one and five years
    after reaching reference age"

F8. The maximum annual tax-deductible pillar 3a contribution for 2025 is CHF 7,258 for
    persons with occupational pension cover, or 20% of earnings up to CHF 36,288 for persons
    without occupational pension cover. (TIME-SENSITIVE: explicitly labelled "in 2025".) —
    evidence quote: "The maximum annual tax-deductible amount in 2025 is: - CHF 7,258 for
    employees/self-employed persons with occupational pension cover; - 20% of earnings up to
    CHF 36,288 for employees/self-employed persions with no occupational pension cover."

F9. Mandatory occupational pension contributions apply above an annual income of CHF 22,680
    (as of 2025) from a single employer, for persons older than 17; the mandatory scheme
    covers annual incomes up to CHF 90,720. (TIME-SENSITIVE: explicitly "as of 2025".) —
    evidence quote: "You are subject to mandatory occupational pension contributions if you
    annually earn more than CHF 22,680 (as of 2025) from one employer and are older than 17
    ... The mandatory insurance scheme covers annual incomes up to CHF 90,720."

F10. Retroactive payment of OASI contribution gaps is possible only for gaps in the previous
    five years. — evidence quote: "this is only possible for gaps which occurred in the
    previous five years and provided that you were insured under the OASI scheme during that
    period. You cannot make up for contribution gaps that date back more than five years."
