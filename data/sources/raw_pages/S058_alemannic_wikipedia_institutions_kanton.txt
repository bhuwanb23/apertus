=== SWISS FACT CONTENT ===
F1. A canton is a member state (Gliedstaat) of the Swiss Confederation; "Stand" and formerly "Ort" are alternative historical names for the same unit. — cross-ref: S025
F2. Switzerland has 26 cantons, and each has its own constitution, parliament, government and courts. — cross-ref: S025
F3. Cantonal parliaments have between 49 and 180 seats; cantonal governments consist of five or seven members depending on the canton. — cross-ref: S008
F4. The federal government retains a say in many matters that are nominally cantonal competences (the article calls this the "Kompetänzwirrwarr", competence confusion). — cross-ref: S025
F5. Only Glarus and Appenzell Innerrhoden still hold an annual Landsgemeinde where the citizenry votes by show of hands. — cross-ref: S040
F6. Jura is the youngest canton, the 23rd, split off from the canton of Bern in 1979. — cross-ref: S030
F7. The six half-cantons each hold one Council of States seat; the other cantons hold two. — cross-ref: S026
F8. Obwalden and Nidwalden are half-cantons although they were never a single canton. — cross-ref: S026
F9. Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden are the founding cantons (Urkantön) that concluded the first alliance in 1291. — cross-ref: S037
F10. Under the Mediation Act of 1803 there were 19 cantons with restored autonomy; after the Congress of Vienna in 1815 there were 22. — cross-ref: S037
