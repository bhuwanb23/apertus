=== SWISS FACT CONTENT ===
F1. A canton is a member state (Gliedstaat) of the Swiss Confederation; 'Stand' and formerly 'Ort' are alternative historical names for the same unit. — cross-ref: S025
F2. The Helvetic Republic abolished the terms 'Stand' and 'Ort' in 1798 and introduced 'Kanton' as the term for the highest administrative unit. — cross-ref: S037
F3. Cantonal parliaments have between 49 and 180 seats; cantonal governments consist of five or seven members depending on the canton. — cross-ref: S008
F4. The federal government retains a say in many matters that are nominally cantonal competences (the article calls this the 'Kompetänzwirrwarr'). — cross-ref: S025
F5. Allies that were not full members of the Confederacy were called zugewandte Orte (associated places). — cross-ref: S037
F6. Only Glarus and Appenzell Innerrhoden still hold an annual Landsgemeinde in which the citizenry votes by show of hands and elects its cantonal representatives. — cross-ref: S040
F7. Jura is the youngest canton, the 23rd, split off from the canton of Bern in 1979. — cross-ref: S030
F8. The six half-cantons each hold one Council of States seat; the other cantons hold two. — cross-ref: S026
F9. Obwalden and Nidwalden are half-cantons although they were never a single canton. — cross-ref: S026
F10. Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden are the founding cantons (Urkantön) that concluded the first alliance in 1291. — cross-ref: S037
