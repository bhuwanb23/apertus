=== SWISS FACT CONTENT ===
F1. The Schweizerisches Idiotikon is a project to inventory all Swiss dialects and the older Swiss chancery and literary language. — cross-ref: S028
F2. Switzerland has four "national dictionaries", one per language region: the Idiotikon (German), the Glossaire des patois de la Suisse romande, the Vocabolario dei dialetti della Svizzera italiana and the Dicziunari Rumantsch Grischun. — cross-ref: S047
F3. "Idiotikon" is an old word for a dialect dictionary; since the middle of the fourth volume (under Albert Bachmann) the work became a full dictionary and the name came to denote the work itself. — cross-ref: none
F4. The first attempt at a Swiss Idiotikon was published by the pastor Franz Joseph Stalder in 1806 in two volumes; his completed 1832 manuscript was never published. — cross-ref: none
F5. The present Schweizerisches Idiotikon was founded in 1862 by the Antiquarische Gesellschaft Zürich and Friedrich (Fritz) Staub as the "Verein für das Schweizerdeutsche Wörterbuch". — cross-ref: none
F6. As of the article's writing (volume 16 finished 2012, volume 17 planned for the letter Z), the dictionary contained about 155,000 headwords. — cross-ref: none
F7. The Idiotikon is the largest dialect dictionary of all Germanic languages and is simultaneously a national historical dictionary comparable to the Oxford English Dictionary or the Swedish Academy's Ordbok. — cross-ref: none
F8. The dictionary is carried by a non-profit association, funded by the Confederation (through the Academy of Humanities and Social Sciences), the German-speaking cantons and private donors — i.e. it is a federal/cantonal co-funded national institution. — cross-ref: S029
F9. It has been readable online at www.idiotikon.ch since September 2010. — cross-ref: none
F10. The dictionary's source material divides into two periods: post-1800 and historical, roughly 1300 to 1799 — documenting that Alemannic dialect forms were written from the 14th century. — cross-ref: S028
