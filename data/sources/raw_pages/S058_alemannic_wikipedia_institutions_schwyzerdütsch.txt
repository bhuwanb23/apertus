=== SWISS FACT CONTENT ===
F1. Switzerland has four 'national dictionaries', one per language region: the Idiotikon (German), the Glossaire des patois de la Suisse romande, the Vocabolario dei dialetti della Svizzera italiana and the Dicziunari Rumantsch Grischun. — cross-ref: S047
F2. 'Idiotikon' is an old word for a dialect dictionary recording region-typical words but not those identical to Standard German. — cross-ref: none
F3. The first attempt at a Swiss Idiotikon was published by the pastor Franz Joseph Stalder in 1806. — cross-ref: none
F4. The present Schweizerisches Idiotikon was founded in 1862 by the Antiquarische Gesellschaft Zürich and Friedrich (Fritz) Staub as the 'Verein für das Schweizerdeutsche Wörterbuch'. — cross-ref: none
F5. Word material was gathered from correspondents all over the country. — cross-ref: none
F6. Volume 16 was only completed in 2012; the printed work runs to 17 volumes, ending with the letter Z. — cross-ref: none
F7. As of volume 16 the dictionary contained about 155,000 headwords. — cross-ref: none
F8. The Idiotikon is the largest dialect dictionary of all Germanic languages. — cross-ref: none
F9. The dictionary is carried by a non-profit association — i.e. a federal/cantonal co-funded national institution. — cross-ref: S029
F10. It has been readable online at www.idiotikon.ch since September 2010. — cross-ref: none
F11. The dictionary divides its source material into two periods: post-1800 and historical, roughly 1300 to 1799 — documenting that Alemannic dialect forms were written from the 14th century. — cross-ref: S028
F12. The last printed volume is to be the 17th, covering the whole letter Z. — cross-ref: none
