=== VERBATIM SOURCE QUOTES ===
"In Switzerland, employers must take out accident insurance for all of their employees.
However, this occupational insurance does not always cover non-occupational accidents."

"If you have full accident cover via your employer, to save on premiums we recommend
excluding accident insurance from your basic health insurance."

"In Switzerland, every individual must be insured against accidents. There are a variety of
ways of doing this:

- If you are a salaried employee, your employer is obliged to take out accident insurance
for you, and a premium is deducted from your salary. The same system applies to employees
who work from home and to trainees.

- If you are registered unemployed, providing you receive unemployment benefits you are
automatically insured via the Swiss National Accident Insurance Fund (Suva) in case of
accident. The premium is deducted from your benefits.

- It you are self-employed you must take out personal accident insurance.

- In all other cases you are not covered by accident insurance specifically. Instead,
accidents are covered as a risk under your compulsory private health insurance. This
applies to stay-at-home partners, children, students and retirees, etc."

"If you work at least eight hours a week for your employer, your accident insurance covers
occupational accidents and illnesses, and non-occupational accidents occurring during your
leisure time.

If you work for less than eight hours a week for your employer, your accident insurance
covers occupational accidents and occupational illnesses only. Accidents that occur on your
way to and from work are considered to be occupational accidents."

"Your accident insurance will reimburse accident-related medical expenses. You do not have
to pay a deductible or retention fee yourself. However, if you are insured against
accidents through your health insurance provider, you will have to pay the deductible and
retention fee."

"If you are unable to work as the result of an accident, as of the third day following your
accident you are entitled to a daily allowance corresponding to 80% of your salary."

"If the allowance paid by your accident insurance is lower than this figure, your employer
must make up the difference. If your insurance benefits correspond to 80% of your salary or
more, your employer does not have to continue paying your salary."

"The insurer may pay you a disability pension if continuing medical treatment does not offer
the prospect of a significant improvement in your health. You must be classified as at least
10% disabled to qualify. This pension may be awarded in addition to a pension under the
state disability insurance (IV/AI) scheme."

"The insurer may also pay you an integrity and/or helplessness allowance if you need
permanent assistance to complete everyday tasks."

"If you have had an accident you must notify your employer as soon as possible. The employer
must in turn notify their insurance company immediately."

=== FACT CANDIDATES (for MCQ generation) ===
F1. Employers in Switzerland are obliged to take out accident insurance for all of their
    employees, and the premium is deducted from the employee's salary. — evidence quote:
    "If you are a salaried employee, your employer is obliged to take out accident
    insurance for you, and a premium is deducted from your salary."

F2. Employees working at least eight hours a week for their employer get cover for
    occupational accidents and illnesses plus non-occupational accidents in leisure time,
    whereas those working under eight hours per week get occupational cover only (commuting
    accidents count as occupational). — evidence quote: "If you work at least eight hours a
    week for your employer, your accident insurance covers occupational accidents and
    illnesses, and non-occupational accidents occurring during your leisure time. If you
    work for less than eight hours a week for your employer, your accident insurance covers
    occupational accidents and occupational illnesses only."

F3. The accident daily allowance equals 80% of salary and becomes payable from the third
    day after the accident. — evidence quote: "as of the third day following your accident
    you are entitled to a daily allowance corresponding to 80% of your salary."

F4. The employer must top up the salary to 80% if the accident insurer's daily allowance is
    lower than 80%, but need not continue paying salary once benefits reach 80% or more. —
    evidence quote: "If the allowance paid by your accident insurance is lower than this
    figure, your employer must make up the difference. If your insurance benefits correspond
    to 80% of your salary or more, your employer does not have to continue paying your
    salary."

F5. An accident disability pension requires a minimum assessed disability of 10% and can be
    paid in addition to the state IV/AI disability pension. — evidence quote: "You must be
    classified as at least 10% disabled to qualify. This pension may be awarded in addition
    to a pension under the state disability insurance (IV/AI) scheme."

F6. Accident-related medical costs are reimbursed without any deductible or retention fee
    where cover comes through accident insurance (unlike cover via health insurance). —
    evidence quote: "You do not have to pay a deductible or retention fee yourself. However,
    if you are insured against accidents through your health insurance provider, you will
    have to pay the deductible and retention fee."

F7. Jobseekers receiving unemployment benefits are automatically accident-insured by the
    Swiss National Accident Insurance Fund (Suva), with the premium deducted from their
    benefits. — evidence quote: "If you are registered unemployed, providing you receive
    unemployment benefits you are automatically insured via the Swiss National Accident
    Insurance Fund (Suva) in case of accident. The premium is deducted from your benefits."

F8. Self-employed persons must arrange their own personal accident insurance. — evidence
    quote: "It you are self-employed you must take out personal accident insurance."
