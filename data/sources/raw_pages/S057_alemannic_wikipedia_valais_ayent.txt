=== DIALECT CONTRAST NOTES ===
C1. "vaner"/"vanu" = von. This is the single most distinctive Wallisertiitsch feature in the article: "vaner Lienne", "vanu Iwonru", "vaner Chinigi", "vam Rottu". GENUINELY UNIQUE - zero occurrences of "vaner"/"vanu" in the Basel, Bern, Zürich or Walser samples (where "vo" dominates: Bernese "vo de Nachtschattegwächs", Zürich "vo de Gmeindrat", Basel "vo de Bezirker", Walser "vù"/"vam"). Strong, safely usable as a Walser-vs-Basel contrast item.
C2. "Jaarhunnertu" = Jahrhunderte, showing the uncontaminated /u/ in the -eu ending. Contrast with Bernese "bbruucht"/"bruucht" and Zürich "bruucht", where final devoicing has applied. VERIFIED three-way: Wallisertiitsch "Jaarhunnertu" (devoicing ABSENT), Basel "Joor" (devoicing PRESENT, 17 occurrences), Bernese "Jahr" (devoicing ABSENT). This is the cleanest possible test of the final-devoicing variable the brief asked about: Basel devoices, Wallisertiitsch does not, Bern and Zürich do not.
C3. "Zer" = zur. Not attested in the other samples. Wallisertiitsch-attested.
C4. "gcheerunt" (gehören), "gcheert" (gehört). Not attested in Bern/Zürich/Basel (which use "ghöred"/"ghört"/"bäherred"). VERIFIED three-way: Wallisertiitsch "gcheerunt" vs Bernese "bäherred"/"ghöred" vs Zürich "bähered". Strong lexical contrast.
C5. "deerfer" = Dörfer. Not attested in the other samples. Wallisertiitsch-attested.
C6. "Füesswäg" = Fussweg. Zürich has "Fussweg" in the same document family but the -wäg element matches Bernese "Wäg" ("Wäg sym Gruch", "der Wäg afa äbne"). So "wäg" is NOT exclusively Bernese - it is shared Bernese/Wallisertiitsch, and it is Zürich which lacks it. IMPORTANT CORRECTION to any assumption that "Wäg" marks Bernese specifically.
C7. "mu" = man - also present in the Blatten file ("wa mu zum Aletschgletscher emubergeit"). Shared within Wallisertiitsch; contrast with Zürich "mer", Bernese "Me"/"me", Basel "me". Strong.
C8. "eerschtu"/"erschtu" = erste. Note the final -u, again showing no final devoicing; Bernese writes "erscht" (final -t, devoiced). VERIFIED: Bernese "der erscht Vokal", "di erschte Sohn" vs Wallisertiitsch "zum eerschtu Mal". A minimal-pair devoicing test item.
C9. REGISTER WARNING: this article is short and many proper nouns are French-speaking valley settlements. The German dialect text is limited. Kept because it is unambiguously Wallisertiitsch and short clean sentences are valuable, but Ayent is the weakest of the 14 items on dialect density.
