=== VERBATIM SOURCE QUOTES ===
"If you are healthy, you can save a great deal on your health insurance premium by choosing
the highest deductible. You should still put CHF 3,200 aside in case you do fall ill
(CHF 2,500 deductible and CHF 700 retention fee)."

"The lowest deductible for adults is CHF 300, the highest CHF 2,500. Deductibles of between
CHF 0 and CHF 600 apply for children."

"The deductible is the amount that you have to pay out of your own pocket towards your
health-related costs every year. The health insurer will not cover costs until this
deductible has been used up."

"Example: You have chosen a deductible of CHF 300 and have to go to the doctor once or
twice a year. Your doctor charges you CHF 200 for each appointment. You will pay all of the
first bill and CHF 100 of the second yourself. Your health insurer will cover CHF 100 of
the second bill, less the retention fee of CHF 10 that you also have to pay (see next
section)."

"When the deductible has been used up you will still pay ten percent of your healthcare
costs. However, this retention fee is limited to CHF 700 per year (CHF 350 for children).
Your annual share of your healthcare costs is therefore capped at your chosen deductible,
plus CHF 700."

"You must pay the contribution to hospital costs if you are hospitalised. It is CHF 15 per
day. The reasoning behind it is that, in hospital, you save costs that you would otherwise
incur at home, such as food and power. Children, young adults in education and training
and pregnant women do not pay any contribution to hospital costs."

"The level of this premium depends on the health insurance company you are with. Other
factors are your insurance model, which deductible you choose and where you live. Premiums
are lower for children and young adults under the age of 25."

"Anyone who is a low earner or has a large number of children may be eligible to have their
premiums subsidised by their canton."

"If you are employed for more than eight hours per week you are covered by compulsory
accident insurance. You do not need to include accident cover additionally in your
compulsory health insurance."

=== FACT CANDIDATES (for MCQ generation) ===
F1. The compulsory health insurance deductible ranges from a minimum of CHF 300 to a maximum
    of CHF 2,500 for adults. (Page states no year for these two endpoints; they are the
    standing statutory bands. Not flagged year-specific by the page itself.) — evidence
    quote: "The lowest deductible for adults is CHF 300, the highest CHF 2,500."

F2. For children, deductibles range from CHF 0 to CHF 600. — evidence quote: "Deductibles of
    between CHF 0 and CHF 600 apply for children."

F3. After the deductible is exhausted, the insured still pays 10% of healthcare costs, but
    the retention fee is capped at CHF 700 per year for adults. — evidence quote: "When the
    deductible has been used up you will still pay ten percent of your healthcare costs.
    However, this retention fee is limited to CHF 700 per year (CHF 350 for children)."

F4. The child's retention fee cap is CHF 350 per year (half the adult cap). — evidence
    quote: "this retention fee is limited to CHF 700 per year (CHF 350 for children)."

F5. The maximum annual own-share of healthcare costs equals the chosen deductible plus
    CHF 700. — evidence quote: "Your annual share of your healthcare costs is therefore
    capped at your chosen deductible, plus CHF 700."

F6. Hospitalised patients pay a hospital contribution of CHF 15 per day, which is waived for
    children, young adults in education or training, and pregnant women. — evidence quote:
    "You must pay the contribution to hospital costs if you are hospitalised. It is CHF 15
    per day. ... Children, young adults in education and training and pregnant women do not
    pay any contribution to hospital costs."

F7. Someone choosing the highest deductible should set aside CHF 3,200 (= CHF 2,500
    deductible + CHF 700 retention fee). — evidence quote: "You should still put CHF 3,200
    aside in case you do fall ill (CHF 2,500 deductible and CHF 700 retention fee)."

F8. Premiums are lower for children and young adults under the age of 25. — evidence quote:
    "Premiums are lower for children and young adults under the age of 25."

F9. Employees working more than eight hours per week are covered by compulsory accident
    insurance, so accident cover need not be duplicated in compulsory health insurance. —
    evidence quote: "If you are employed for more than eight hours per week you are covered
    by compulsory accident insurance. You do not need to include accident cover additionally
    in your compulsory health insurance."
