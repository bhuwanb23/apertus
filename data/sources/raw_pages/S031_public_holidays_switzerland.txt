=== FACT CANDIDATES (for MCQ generation) ===
F1. 1 August (Swiss National Day) is the only nationwide/national public holiday in Switzerland; there is no federal statutory list of other national holidays. — evidence quote: "The first of August – Swiss national day – is Switzerland's only national public holiday."

F2. CANTONAL/COMMUNAL VARIABLE — Cantons may add up to eight further public holidays beyond 1 August, so the holiday calendar differs from canton to canton (including within-canton variation by commune). — evidence quote: "The cantons can each have up to eight additional public holidays on their territory. Public holidays can thus vary from canton to canton."

F3. A public holiday that falls on a non-working day (e.g. Saturday or Sunday) is not compensable as time off during that week. — evidence quote: "If a public holiday falls on a non-working day, such as a Saturday or Sunday, you cannot redeem the holiday time during the week."

F4. A public holiday falling during annual leave does not consume a day of the employee's vacation entitlement. — evidence quote: "If a public holiday falls during your vacation, that day does not count as a day of vacation."

F5. Minimum statutory annual leave is 4 weeks for all workers (full- and part-time), rising to at least 5 weeks for workers under 20. — evidence quote: "Regardless of whether you work full or part-time, you are entitled to at least four weeks' vacation per year." / "Up to the age of 20, you are entitled to at least five weeks' vacation."

F6. Taking time off for the listed special events (court appearance, public duties, marriage, death of a close relative, moving house, birth of a child, caring for a close relative, looking after a sick child) does not require making up the hours/days. — evidence quote: "In the cases listed below, you can take time off work without having to make up the hours/days that you are absent from work" (list includes "A court appearance or similar legal appointment", "Public duties (working as a member of Parliament, local councillor, etc.)", "Marriage", "Death of a close relative", "Moving house", "Birth of your child", "Care of a close relative", "Looking after a sick child").

F7. The duration of leave for special absences is NOT fixed by law — it is left to company rules. — evidence quote: "The law does not clearly define how many hours or days you can take off. Ask your employer about the rules that apply in your company."
