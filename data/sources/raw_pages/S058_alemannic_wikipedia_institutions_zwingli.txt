=== SWISS FACT CONTENT ===
F1. Zwingli's theological position is called Zwinglianism. — cross-ref: none
F2. Zwingli and the Genevan Calvin are the two founders of the Reformed Church in Switzerland. — cross-ref: none
F3. Zwingli grew up in Wildhaus in the Toggenburg and was a pastor in Glarus from 1506 — the dialectal 'Ueli' for Ulrich marks his Glarus period. — cross-ref: none
F4. The Glarus Landsgemeinde approved Zwingli's proposal in 1510 to found a Latin school, i.e. a Gymnasium — a Swiss civic-democratic institution deciding education policy. — cross-ref: S033
F5. Zwingli was called to Zurich in 1519 as people's priest at the Grossmünster. — cross-ref: none
F6. The three great Zurich Disputations of 1523 and 1524 were public theological disputations of the Swiss Reformation. — cross-ref: none
F7. Following Zwingli's preaching the Zurich council banned the Catholic Mass and enacted new church laws. — cross-ref: none
F8. Between 1524 and 1529 Zwingli and Leo Jud translated the Bible, producing the first edition of the Zürcher Bibel. — cross-ref: none
F9. The Central Swiss cantons refused to join Zwingli's 'Trybe' (the Protestant League), which led to the Second Kappelerkrieg. — cross-ref: none
F10. Zwingli died in the Battle of Kappel on 11 October 1531. — cross-ref: none
F11. Heinrich Bullinger succeeded Zwingli in Zurich. — cross-ref: none
F12. Zwingli was more radical than Luther. — cross-ref: none
F13. Zwingli opposed the Reisläuferei, the Swiss practice of selling Swiss soldiers as mercenaries to foreign wars. — cross-ref: none
F14. (Not carried by a harvested sentence, but present in the article text.) Zwingli's secularisation of monasteries and transfer of their assets to the state is credited with paving the way for the modern welfare state — a distinctly Swiss Reformation fact. — cross-ref: none
