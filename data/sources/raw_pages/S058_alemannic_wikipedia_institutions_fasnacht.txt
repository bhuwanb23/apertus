=== SWISS FACT CONTENT ===
F1. Of the Swiss Protestant (reformed) regions, only Basel and its countryside preserved the Fasnacht through the Reformation. — cross-ref: none
F2. The Basel Fasnacht runs for three days. — cross-ref: none
F3. The Laufenburg town Fasnacht (Städtlefasnacht) is documented since the year 1386. — cross-ref: none
F4. At the third "Fassel" the Laufenburg Fasnacht deposes the mayor, and the same day features the "Salm-Alandig" — and the Fasnacht then crosses the cantonal border between the two Laufenburgs. — cross-ref: none
F5. Solothurn Fasnacht participants nickname their city "Honolulu" during the carnival season, a usage dating from the 19th-century journal "Dr Poschtheiri". — cross-ref: none
F6. The "Naarezumft Honolulu" was founded in 1853 to organise the Solothurn Fasnacht. — cross-ref: none
F7. Solothurn's official carnival start is 13 January ("Hilari"), not 11 November — unlike other carnival traditions. — cross-ref: none
F8. Basel carnival specialities include Mehlsuppe with onion tart (Ziibelewaie) and cheese tart (Chäswaie). — cross-ref: none
F9. The Swabian-Alemannic Fasnet is listed by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage. — cross-ref: none
F10. Fasnacht traditions now survive in many Swiss cantons. — cross-ref: none
